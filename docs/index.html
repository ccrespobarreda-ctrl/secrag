Lululemon recognizes revenue when performance obligations are satisfied through the transfer of control of promised goods or services to customers, meaning once the customer has the ability to direct the use of, and obtain substantially all of the benefits from, the product—including transfer of legal title, physical possession, risks and rewards of ownership, and customer acceptance [1][2]. Specifically, revenue from company-operated stores and other retail locations is recognized at the point of sale, while e-commerce revenue, sales to wholesale accounts, and in-home fitness hardware sales are recognized upon receipt by the customer [1][2]. In arrangements where the Company receives payment before the customer receives the promised good, the amount is initially recorded as deferred revenue and recognized as revenue only when control transfers to the customer [1][2]. All net revenue is reported net of markdowns and discounts, sales taxes collected on behalf of taxing authorities, and returns, with revenue also presented net of an allowance for estimated returns [1][2]. Proceeds from gift card sales are deferred and recognized as revenue when the gift card is tendered for payment, with unredeemed balances recognized under a redemption recognition method [2][4].
