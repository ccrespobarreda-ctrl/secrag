Asked 16 questions it could not answer, it invented 1.

Question answering over 4,169 chunks of SEC annual reports, with citations verified in code. The hard part is not producing fluent answers; it is not producing them when the documents do not support one. Sixteen of the fifty questions have no answer in the corpus and five are written to bait an invention — a fiscal year outside the filings, a business segment that does not exist. Across 3 runs, 2.1% of responses to those questions were classified as answering, and the adversarial five produced none.

The cost is refusing 5.9% of the questions it could have answered. That is bounded by retrieval, not by caution: the retriever surfaces a correct chunk 88% of the time, and where it does not, refusing is the right answer.

Lululemon recognizes revenue when performance obligations are satisfied through the transfer of control of promised goods or services to customers, meaning the customer has the ability to direct the use of, and obtain substantially all of the benefits from, the product—including transfer of legal title, physical possession, risks and rewards of ownership, and customer acceptance [1][2]. Revenue from company-operated stores and other retail locations is recognized at the point of sale [1][2]. E-commerce revenue, sales to wholesale accounts, and in-home fitness hardware sales are recognized upon receipt by the customer [1][2]. When payment is received before the customer receives the promised good, the payment is initially recorded as deferred revenue and recognized as revenue when control transfers to the customer [1][2]. All revenue is reported net of markdowns and discounts, sales taxes collected on behalf of taxing authorities, and returns, and is presented net of an allowance for estimated returns [1][2].

La red logistica de Wayfair consta de CastleGate y la Wayfair Delivery Network (WDN), mas CastleGate Forwarding para servicios de entrada. CastleGate permite a los proveedores posicionar inventario por adelantado en sus almacenes, con entrega en dos dias o menos a la mayoria de la poblacion de EE UU. El metodo principal de cumplimiento es una red de envio directo integrada con la tecnologia de los proveedores.

Wayfair's logistics network was built specifically for the home category, where items can be bulky, heavy, and prone to damage, with its primary fulfillment method being a drop-ship network integrated with suppliers' back-end technology to send order information directly to a supplier's warehouse [1][11]. Delivery is made primarily through FedEx for small parcel products, and through third-party line haul trucking companies and last mile home delivery agents for large parcel products [1][11][10]. The company has grown the percentage of orders shipped from its CastleGate warehouses, supported by CastleGate Forwarding (CGF) services offering inbound freight, drayage, and ocean services for suppliers shipping from Asia directly to CastleGate locations [1][4]. The majority of large parcel items are delivered through the Wayfair Delivery Network (WDN), which includes consolidation centers, cross docks, and last mile delivery facilities [1][4]. Wayfair believes its proprietary logistics network helps drive incremental sales through faster delivery times, lower prices, and a better home delivery experience, and for CastleGate-shipped items it can deliver many products to a majority of the U.S. population in two days or less [1][13]. The company also notes it primarily relies on a single carrier, FedEx, for small parcel delivery and on third-party national, regional, and local transportation companies for large parcel deliveries, which exposes it to risks beyond its control such as weather, labor issues, and supply chain disruptions [10].

Abercrombie & Fitch identifies significant uncertainty from changing tariff rates and shifting U.S. trade policies, including retaliatory tariffs imposed by other countries on U.S. exports, which could disrupt global trade flows and adversely affect the business [1]. The company notes that increased or maintained elevated tariffs on goods from sourcing countries would raise merchandise costs relative to typical costs, potentially harming margins and consumer demand [1]. It also flags risks of incorrectly anticipating trade policy outcomes, missing business opportunities, or failing to adapt strategies effectively [6]. Notably, tariffs on imported goods negatively impacted operating income by $90 million, or 170 basis points as a percent of net sales, during Fiscal 2025, and the company expects an additional approximately $40 million incremental impact (about 70 basis points) in Fiscal 2026 from a 15% tariff on all U.S. imports [2][7]. As a subsequent event, the company disclosed that the U.S. Supreme Court struck down certain IEEPA tariffs in February 2026 and a new 10% global tariff under Section 122 of the Trade Act was announced, with uncertain impact including possible tariff refunds [3]. To mitigate these risks, the company describes a "playbook" of strategies including evaluating supply chain footprint changes, negotiating with vendors, managing product costs, pursuing average unit retail (AUR) growth, and considering operating expense reductions, while acknowledging these efforts may not fully offset tariff impacts [1][7].
